======================================================================

PURPOSE AND SCOPE
======================================================================

This document specifies a proposed integrated semantic architecture connecting:

Universal Humanoid Ontology (UHO)
Universal Humanoid Manipulation Ontology (UHMO)
ROBOPARTS.ai physical component and lifecycle infrastructure
ROBOTRAITS.ai personality and behavioral ontology
RoboAgentic.ai intelligent-agent architecture
PITN.ai research and intelligence infrastructure
The architecture is intended to establish a common, machine-readable representation of robotic identity, physical configuration, capabilities, behavioral characteristics, task requirements, maintenance, commercial relationships, digital identity, and lifecycle events.

The system separates the description of a robot from the software that reasons about the robot and from the mechanisms that control physical hardware.

The ontology is intended to support:

Robotics component discovery
Humanoid hardware classification
Actuator and end-effector compatibility
Robotic hand capability descriptions
Personality-configured intelligent agents
Capability assessment and task matching
Digital twins and knowledge graphs
Component provenance and lifecycle tracking
Predictive maintenance research
Human-robot interaction
Research reproducibility
Marketplace and supplier integration
Versioned software interoperability
This document describes a proposed architecture. Production readiness, interoperability, safety performance, commercial viability, and legal ownership must be established independently through implementation, testing, and appropriate review.

======================================================================

CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

2.1 Persistent Identity

Every robot, component, assembly, software module, personality profile, trait, task, organization, maintenance event, and digital record should have a stable identifier.

Identifiers must not depend exclusively on a mutable product name, manufacturer description, blockchain address, or database row number.

2.2 Shared Entities

An entity should be defined once and referenced by other modules.

For example, an actuator installed in a robotic hand should retain its component identity when referenced by the hardware ontology, maintenance system, marketplace, digital twin, or capability assessment.

2.3 Typed Relationships

Relationships must have defined meanings, valid source types, valid target types, and documented cardinality where applicable.

Examples:

Robot HASPART Component Component REQUIRES Component Robot HASCAPABILITY Capability Trait INFLUENCES BehavioralPolicy Task REQUIRES Capability MaintenanceEvent AFFECTS Component DigitalTwin REPRESENTS Robot

2.4 Evidence-Based Capabilities

The system must distinguish:

Declared capability
Manufacturer specification
Simulated capability
Laboratory-tested capability
Field-validated capability
Currently available capability
Unverified capability
A specification alone must not be treated as proof that a complete robot can reliably perform a task.

2.5 Separation of Concerns

Personality configuration, reasoning, learning, task planning, physical control, and safety enforcement are separate architectural responsibilities.

Personality may influence permitted behavioral choices. It must not override physical limits, authorization, emergency controls, or independent safety mechanisms.

2.6 Versioning and Provenance

Records should include their version, source, verification state, and relevant change history.

2.7 Extensibility

New ontology domains should be added without requiring all existing identifiers, records, or relationships to be redesigned.

3.6 HCO — Humanoid Commerce Ontology

3.7 HDO — Humanoid Digital Ontology

Purpose: Represents digital twins, technical documentation, CAD assets, software records, knowledge graphs, model artifacts, product catalogs, and APIs.

Purpose: Represents exterior and human-facing components separately from functional robotics components.

Examples:

Synthetic skin
Surface coverings
Artificial hair
Artificial nails
Facial coverings
Texture and color layers
Human-facing interface materials
These entities may attach to a robotic body part but must not automatically be classified as mechanical or manipulation capabilities.

3.9 ROBOTRAITS Ontology

Purpose: Defines machine-readable traits, personality archetypes, behavioral preferences, cognitive styles, interaction policies, and adaptation metadata.

3.10 RoboAgentic.ai

Purpose: Provides the proposed intelligent-agent layer that can consume robot descriptions, task context, authorized memory, behavioral profiles, and verified capability information.

3.11 PITN.ai

Purpose: Provides the overarching research and intelligence infrastructure through which the proposed modules may interoperate.

The names and boundaries above are architectural definitions for this specification. Their use does not by itself establish ownership, exclusivity, registration, or legal protection.

======================================================================

CANONICAL ENTITY MODEL
======================================================================

Every principal ontology record should support a common metadata structure.

Required fields:

entityid entitytype schema_version name description

Recommended fields:

attributes relationships source provenance verificationstatus createdat updatedat revision lifecyclestatus ownerreference accessclassification

Field definitions:

entity_id: Persistent identifier within the applicable identifier namespace.

entity_type: Canonical type of entity.

schema_version: Version of the schema used to validate the record.

name: Human-readable label.

description: Concise definition of the entity.

attributes: Type-specific values, measurements, and configuration fields.

relationships: References to other entities using persistent identifiers.

source: Origin of the record or information.

provenance: Evidence or record of how the information was obtained.

verification_status: State indicating whether information is unverified, reviewed, tested, or independently validated.

revision: Revision of the individual record.

lifecycle_status: Current lifecycle state, where applicable.

owner_reference: Reference to the entity or organization responsible for the record, where established.

access_classification: Access policy for the record.

Ownership, provenance, verification, and access permissions are distinct concepts. A source record does not necessarily establish legal ownership, and a verified technical claim does not establish intellectual-property rights.

ROBOTRAITS DOMAIN DEFINITION
======================================================================

Domain: Artificial Personality Systems for Intelligent Robotics.

ROBOTRAITS is a proposed domain ontology for representing computational personality configurations, behavioral traits, cognitive processing styles, interaction preferences, and context-sensitive behavioral policies in intelligent robotic agents.

It may integrate:

Symbolic reasoning
Machine learning
Reinforcement learning
Foundation models
Psychometrics-informed models
Natural-language processing
Human-robot interaction
Context and memory
Environmental sensing
Capability-aware task planning
The ontology describes data and semantic relationships. It does not independently execute an AI model, establish consciousness, or guarantee that a robot behaves according to a personality label.

5.1 ROBOTRAITS Core Entities

PersonalityProfile PersonalityArchetype BehavioralTrait TraitMeasurement BehavioralPreference CognitiveStyle InteractionPolicy BehavioralPolicy ContextCondition TaskObjective FeedbackRecord LearningRecord ModelVersion SafetyConstraint CapabilityRecord UserPreference EvaluationResult

5.2 Trait Categories

Cognitive: Reasoning style, planning preference, abstraction, forecasting, memory use.

Analytical: Verification, critical analysis, evidence evaluation, precision.

Creative: Idea generation, alternative exploration, flexibility, originality.

Social: Cooperation, social awareness, conversational engagement.

Communication: Formality, verbosity preference, turn-taking, explanation style.

Operational: Organization, execution preference, resource management.

Technical: Diagnostic approach, engineering focus, system analysis.

Adaptive: Response to feedback, context adjustment, strategy revision.

Emotional-response modeling: Computational representations of response tendencies, affective cues, and interaction state. These must not be treated as proof of subjective emotional experience.

Protective: Caution, risk awareness, protective task preferences.

Ethical and governance: Policy compliance, authorization awareness, privacy and consent requirements.

Ethical and safety requirements must also exist as enforceable policy constraints independent of personality trait values.

======================================================================

TRAIT DATA MODEL
======================================================================

Each trait should be represented as a structured record.

Required fields:

traitid traitname traitcategory definition valuetype schema_version

Recommended fields:

value unit minimumvalue maximumvalue confidence measurementmethod context activationconditions behavioraleffects constraints provenance verificationstatus revision

Trait values must have explicit semantics.

For example, a numeric value between 0.0 and 1.0 is meaningful only if the schema defines what the endpoints mean and how the value is measured.

A confidence score is not the same as a trait value.

A measurement result is not automatically a verified behavioral capability.

A personality trait is not a safety authorization.

PERSONALITY ARCHETYPES
======================================================================

Archetypes are configurable collections of traits and behavioral preferences. They are not fixed identities, diagnoses, or guarantees of competence.

The initial archetype vocabulary includes:

Strategic Mastermind
Traits: systems thinking, forecasting, long-term planning. Functions: strategic analysis, planning, architecture evaluation.

Analytical Researcher
Traits: curiosity, abstraction, evidence evaluation. Functions: research, simulation, knowledge synthesis.

Creative Innovator
Traits: originality, flexibility, alternative exploration. Functions: idea generation, design exploration.

Human-Centered Visionary
Traits: empathy-oriented interaction, human-context awareness. Functions: assistance, relationship modeling, accessible communication.

Creative Individualist
Traits: creative expression, imagination, customization. Functions: personalized interaction and creative generation.

Collaborative Mentor
Traits: communication, cooperation, explanatory behavior. Functions: education, coaching, collaborative assistance.

Enthusiastic Explorer
Traits: curiosity, discovery, possibility exploration. Functions: brainstorming and exploratory research.

Precision Guardian
Traits: verification, discipline, consistency. Functions: checking, auditing, process compliance.

Protective Caregiver
Traits: support, caution, service orientation. Functions: authorized assistance and protective workflows.

Operational Organizer
Traits: organization, resource management, efficiency. Functions: scheduling and operational coordination.

Community Coordinator
Traits: cooperation, social awareness, coordination. Functions: communication and community support.

Technical Engineer
Traits: diagnostics, practical analysis, technical adaptability. Functions: troubleshooting and engineering assistance.

Adaptive Artist
Traits: creativity, sensory awareness, flexibility. Functions: artistic interaction and environmental adaptation.

Autonomous Operator
Traits: action orientation, situational responsiveness, risk awareness. Functions: authorized real-time task coordination.

Social Companion
Traits: conversational engagement, expressive interaction, social responsiveness. Functions: companionship-oriented interaction and entertainment.

Additional archetypes may be added through schema-governed extensions.

The source material also describes leadership, execution, innovation, human-centered reasoning, and other trait combinations. These should be represented as configurable profiles or additional explicitly defined archetypes rather than assigned undocumented identities.

Psychometric associations must be treated as optional model metadata. Human personality frameworks must not be interpreted as direct measurements of robotic consciousness or assumed to transfer unchanged from humans to machines.

8.2 BASIC VALIDATION

ALLOWEDVERIFICATIONSTATES = { "unverified", "declared", "reviewed", "tested", "validated", "rejected", }

ALLOWEDTRAITCATEGORIES = { "cognitive", "analytical", "creative", "social", "communication", "operational", "technical", "adaptive", "emotionalresponse", "protective", "ethicalgovernance", }

def validate_entity(entity: OntologyEntity) -> list[str]: errors = []

if not entity.entityid.strip(): errors.append("entityid is required")

if not entity.entitytype.strip(): errors.append("entitytype is required")

if not entity.name.strip(): errors.append("name is required")

if entity.revision < 1: errors.append("revision must be at least 1")

if entity.verificationstatus not in ALLOWEDVERIFICATIONSTATES: errors.append("invalid verificationstatus")

return errors

def validate_trait(trait: BehavioralTrait) -> list[str]: errors = []

if not trait.traitid.strip(): errors.append("traitid is required")

if not trait.traitname.strip(): errors.append("traitname is required")

if trait.traitcategory not in ALLOWEDTRAITCATEGORIES: errors.append("unsupported traitcategory")

if not trait.definition.strip(): errors.append("trait definition is required")

if trait.confidence is not None: if not 0.0 <= trait.confidence <= 1.0: errors.append("confidence must be between 0.0 and 1.0")

if trait.value_type == "float" and trait.value is not None: if isinstance(trait.value, bool): errors.append("boolean is not a valid float trait value") elif not isinstance(trait.value, (float, int)): errors.append("trait value must be numeric")

return errors

def validatearchetype( archetype: PersonalityArchetype, knowntrait_ids: set[str], ) -> list[str]: errors = []

if not archetype.archetypeid.strip(): errors.append("archetypeid is required")

if not archetype.name.strip(): errors.append("archetype name is required")

for traitid, weight in archetype.traitweights.items(): if traitid not in knowntraitids: errors.append(f"unknown trait reference: {traitid}")

if not 0.0 <= weight <= 1.0: errors.append( f"weight for {trait_id} must be between 0.0 and 1.0" )

return errors

ROBOT IDENTITY AND HARDWARE CONFIGURATION
======================================================================

A robot profile should reference the actual or declared hardware configuration.

Example entity relationships:

Robot HASPART -> ArmAssembly HASPART -> HandAssembly HASPART -> SensorSystem HASPART -> Controller HASCONFIGURATION -> ConfigurationRecord HASCAPABILITY -> CapabilityRecord HASBEHAVIORALPROFILE -> PersonalityProfile HASDIGITALTWIN -> DigitalTwin

A component record should include, where applicable:

componentid componenttype manufacturerreference manufacturerpartnumber revision serialnumber dimensions mass material powerrequirements mechanicalinterfaces electricalinterfaces communicationinterfaces operatinglimits servicelife maintenanceinterval lifecyclestatus verification_status

Not every field applies to every component. Component-type schemas should define which fields are required.

A robotic hand should not be considered compatible with an arm solely because their product categories match. Compatibility requires assessment of mounting, load, electrical, communication, control, physical-envelope, and other relevant requirements.

======================================================================

CAPABILITY AND TASK MATCHING
======================================================================

A capability record describes what a robot or component is claimed or demonstrated to support.

Suggested fields:

capabilityid capabilitytype description requiredcomponents requiredsoftware operatingconditions performancemetrics evidencereferences verificationstatus revision

A task requirement describes what must be satisfied for a task to be considered suitable.

Suggested fields:

taskid requiredcapabilities minimumperformance environmentalconditions physicalconstraints interactionconstraints authorizationrequirements requiredevidence

The matching system should distinguish:

MATCH: Available evidence supports all required criteria.

PARTIAL_MATCH: Some criteria are supported, while others remain unresolved.

INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE: The required information or test evidence is missing.

INCOMPATIBLE: A known constraint or dependency conflicts with the requirement.

REQUIRES_VALIDATION: A proposed configuration may be suitable but requires additional testing.

These results are decision-support outputs, not a substitute for engineering approval or safety validation.

======================================================================

HARDWARE AND BEHAVIORAL INTEGRATION
======================================================================

The integrated system connects physical capability to behavioral selection through explicit interfaces.

Proposed flow:

PHYSICAL CONFIGURATION | v COMPONENT AND SENSOR RECORDS | v CAPABILITY ASSESSMENT | v ROBOTRAITS PROFILE | v TASK AND CONTEXT EVALUATION | v AUTHORIZED BEHAVIOR SELECTION | v EXISTING ROBOT CONTROL SYSTEM | v OBSERVATION AND PERFORMANCE RECORD | v VALIDATION AND APPROVED ADAPTATION

The ontology does not itself control motors, actuators, brakes, or emergency stops.

Personality profiles may influence planning preferences, interaction styles, or permitted behavioral alternatives. They must not directly modify safety-critical control limits.

Hardware changes must trigger reassessment of affected capability records where the change could affect performance, compatibility, or operating constraints.

======================================================================

MAINTENANCE AND LIFECYCLE
======================================================================

Maintenance event categories include:

Inspection Cleaning Calibration Lubrication Repair Replacement FirmwareUpdate SoftwareUpdate PerformanceTest Refurbishment Upgrade Recycling Retirement

Each maintenance event should reference:

eventid componentid robotid eventtype timestamp procedurereference technicianreference result cost evidencereference configurationrevision remainingusefullife_estimate

Remaining useful life is an estimate unless supported by an appropriate validated method.

Predictive maintenance should record model versions, input data references, uncertainty, and evaluation results. Predictions must not be represented as guaranteed outcomes.

DIGITAL IDENTITY AND WEB3 INTEROPERABILITY
======================================================================

The architecture may support decentralized identifiers, verifiable credentials, blockchain-based provenance, or other decentralized identity technologies through optional adapters.

Suggested identity fields:

digitalidentityid entityid identitymethod externalidentifier issuerreference credentialreference verificationstatus created_at revision

A blockchain address must not be treated as proof of legal ownership, component authenticity, technical competence, or authorization without an applicable verification process.

Sensitive personal information, confidential robot telemetry, credentials, and private operational records should not be placed on a public blockchain merely to make them traceable.

Web3 integration is optional. The core ontology should remain usable without a blockchain network.

======================================================================

SAFETY, SECURITY, AND GOVERNANCE
======================================================================

Safety and security constraints apply independently of personality and learning.

Required design principles:

Independent emergency stopping appropriate to the robot's hazard analysis.
Hardware-appropriate safe-state management.
Authentication and authorization for privileged operations.
Validated command interfaces.
Physical and operational limit enforcement.
Secure software updates.
Configuration and model version tracking.
Audit records for safety-relevant events.
Controlled deployment and rollback.
Testing after relevant hardware or software changes.
Privacy and consent controls for interaction data.
Separation between proposed behavior and approved action.
An emergency stop must not rely exclusively on an AI model, network connection, remote service, or personality configuration.

The correct stopping behavior depends on the robot and its hazards. Simply removing all power can be unsafe for some systems.

AI-generated assessments must be distinguishable from verified engineering records.

Accuracy of compatibility assessments.
Detection of missing dependencies.

Maintenance record completeness.
Rate of incorrect capability claims.

IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP
======================================================================

Phase 1 — Canonical Ontology

Define identifiers, entity types, relationship types, required fields, validation rules, and schema versions.

Phase 2 — Hardware and Capability Registry

Implement robots, components, assemblies, interfaces, technical specifications, and evidence-linked capability records.

Phase 3 — ROBOTRAITS

Implement trait definitions, archetype profiles, context conditions, behavioral preferences, and versioned evaluation records.

Phase 4 — Compatibility and Task Matching

Implement rule-based compatibility checks, explicit missing-information reports, and evidence-aware task-suitability assessments.

Phase 5 — Lifecycle and Commerce

Connect component changes, maintenance events, product listings, suppliers, quotations, and lifecycle costs.

Phase 6 — Knowledge Graph and Digital Twins

Introduce graph storage or graph-query infrastructure when demonstrated requirements justify it. A relational database can provide the initial implementation.

Phase 7 — AI and Adaptive Learning

Integrate approved reasoning, machine-learning, and learning systems through controlled interfaces. Record model versions, evaluation results, permissions, and rollback information.

Phase 8 — Interoperability and Research

Test the ontology across multiple robotic platforms and publish reproducible evaluation results.

FINAL ARCHITECTURAL DEFINITION
======================================================================

The proposed PITN.ai integrated robotics architecture connects physical robot identity, hardware configuration, manipulation systems, measurable capabilities, behavioral traits, task requirements, lifecycle records, digital representations, and commercial relationships through a shared semantic model.

UHO provides the common humanoid robotics vocabulary.

UHMO describes manipulation, actuation, robotic hands, and end effectors.

HRO describes physical hardware and configuration.

HMO describes maintenance and lifecycle events.

HEO describes economic attributes.

HCO describes commercial relationships.

HDO describes digital records and representations.

CHIO describes cosmetic and human-facing exterior systems.

ROBOTRAITS describes computational personality profiles, behavioral traits, cognitive styles, and interaction preferences.

RoboAgentic.ai represents the proposed intelligent-agent layer.

ROBOPARTS.ai represents the proposed physical component and lifecycle infrastructure.

PITN.ai provides the overarching research and intelligence framework.

The central architectural principle is that physical identity, demonstrated capability, behavioral configuration, and task suitability must be connected through explicit relationships and evidence while remaining separate from the mechanisms that enforce physical safety and authorize real-world action.

The intended outcome is a modular, extensible, evidence-aware software architecture for connecting heterogeneous robotic systems through consistent machine-readable descriptions.
